## 1. Causal re-evaluation of the Week 1 analysis

### Why

Every figure in `WEEK1_REPORT.md` came from `gate_analysis.py`, which scans whole files
with full future context. A deployed detector at time *t* sees only data up to *t*. The
revised proposal lists **"Week 1 separation does not survive causal evaluation"** as a
High risk, and it was the cheapest High risk on the board to retire — the data was already
on disk.

### What was built

`causal_eval.py`. Two things made causal:

1. **Smoothing.** `gate_analysis.report_matched_pairs` uses `rolling(center=True)`, which
   averages over frames that have not happened yet. Trailing means only.
2. **Detection time.** The offline slip feature returns the largest drop anywhere in the
   file. A detection now has a *moment* — the first frame the score crosses threshold —
   which is what lead time must be measured from.

### Result — the rule survives

| window | seconds | clean max | slip min | margin | verdict | slips scored |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 0.10 | 10 | 316 | 307 | SEPARATED | 3/3 |
| 5 | 0.17 | 8 | 316 | 308 | SEPARATED | 3/3 |
| **10** | **0.33** | **6** | **316** | **310** | **SEPARATED** | **3/3** |
| 15 | 0.50 | 5 | 260 | 255 | SEPARATED | 3/3 |
| 30 | 1.00 | 0 | 0 | 0 | overlaps | 2/3 |
| 150 | 5.00 | 0 | 0 | 0 | overlaps | 0/3 |

Offline separation was 305–386 against 0–17; causal at the best window is 316–365 against
0–6. **The High risk row can be closed.** The reason it survives is worth putting in the
paper: the drop feature never needed future context — it looks backwards by construction.
What the offline implementation was really using the future for was the *command*
condition, and that turns out not to matter here.

**The collision figures were not reading the future either.** Matched pairs re-run with
trailing instead of centred smoothing gave 27.5× / 54.2× / 273.3× against centred
27.5× / 56.6× / 273.3×, with the peak moving +0.13s later in all three — half the window,
exactly what a trailing mean must do.

### Surprise 1 — window length is bounded ABOVE by grasp duration

This inverts the assumption taken from the industrial AD literature (0.78 AUROC at
500-sample windows, below 0.4 at short ones), which had been read as "`SMOOTH_FRAMES = 5`
is far too short".

Past ~15 frames the rule does not degrade gracefully — it **stops arming altogether** and
scores exactly 0. A trailing mean longer than the grasp dilutes held current below the 150
threshold, so the detector never sees a grasp to lose. `slip_b` drops out first; its grasp
is only ~11 frames (0.37s).

The 500-sample result concerns a *learned autoencoder over a window*. That is the D0+
rung, and the long-window finding should be tested there, where it belongs. For D0's
conditioned rule, 5–10 frames is not a lazy default — it is near-optimal.

**Action taken:** corpus rollouts should record grasp duration per episode, so this bound
can be stated from data rather than from three runs.

### Surprise 2 — smoothing costs lead time, and there is little to spend

`slip_b` leads by +0.07s unsmoothed, +0.03s at window 5, and **−0.07s at window 10** —
firing *after* the operator keypress. The window that maximises margin is not the window
that maximises lead, and against a total budget of 0.3–1.1s that trade is real.

Pick the operating point on lead time, not separation, and report both.

**Do not quote `slip_a`'s +5.11s.** The detector fires on the first, unmarked slip at
~7.3s while the keypress belongs to the second slip at 12.47s. That is the unprompted true
positive from `WEEK1_REPORT.md §1`, not five seconds of lead.

### A bug that nearly became a false result

The first implementation smoothed the current channel but checked goal-stationarity over
an *unsmoothed* interval. The two desynchronize by the window lag, so a deliberate release
passes the filter — the command finishes moving before the smoothed current gets around to
falling. Clean runs scored 258–305 against slips at 260–349: complete overlap, and it
looked exactly like the rule failing under causal evaluation.

It was caught only because window=1 reproduced the offline numbers exactly, which made no
sense if the rule were genuinely non-causal. Fix: widen the goal check to cover the
anchor's own smoothing window. Both the symptom and the fix are in the function docstring,
because in a paper this would be indistinguishable from a real negative result.

## 2. Free-space current model (D0r)

### Why

The matched-pair method that produced Week 1's collision numbers is an experimental
technique, not a detector. It works by diffing against a clear run of the identical
trajectory, and at runtime there is no twin. This was the largest gap Week 1 left.

FACTR 2 (arXiv 2606.12406) names the problem exactly — joint current conflates free-space
actuator effort with contact-induced torque, and separating them needs temporal context —
and validates the fix: replace the *physical* twin with a *predicted* one.

    residual(t) = measured current(t) − predicted free-space current(t)

### Data collected

Two runs of deliberate no-contact teleoperation, ~57s each, 3392 frames total:
`freespace_01.csv` (no payload) and `freespace_payload_01.csv` (holding an object).
Workspace coverage 1400–1700 ticks per major joint.

### Design decisions

**Linear model, not a GBM or a network.** No sklearn in the environment, and adding a
dependency to a rung whose entire claim is being cheap is the wrong trade. What is fitted
is the classic rigid-body regressor — `τ ≈ M(q)q̈ + C(q,q̇)q̇ + g(q) + friction` — over a
physically motivated basis, ridge-solved in closed form with numpy. One dot product per
control step, readable coefficients, saved as `.npz`.

**Features are commanded, never measured.** Everything derives from `goal_pos.*`. Feeding
in measured position would hand the model following error — a direct contact signal — and
it would learn to explain contact away, flattening the residual exactly where it is
needed. Conditioning on the command also keeps inputs in-distribution during a fault, so
the excess is clean rather than an extrapolation artefact.

**Training data must contain no contact.** This is a normality model; contact is by
construction the thing it cannot explain. `fit` refuses files whose names look like faults.

**Configuration-dependent inertia terms added.** `shoulder_pan` rotates about the vertical
and therefore has no gravity term at all — its current is friction and inertia only, and it
was much the worst-fit joint, *and* it is the joint that collides. Adding terms for
effective inertia scaling with arm extension improved held-out MAE on every joint.

### Result — the collision signature survives without a matched control

At the p99 floor:

| run | × floor | peak t | matched-pair ground truth | joint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `pair1_obstacle` | 3.3 | 7.95s | 7.95s | shoulder_pan |
| `pair2_obstacle` | 3.9 | 9.06s | 8.86s | shoulder_pan |
| `pair3_obstacle` | 28.4 | 7.42s | 7.39s | shoulder_pan |
| `pair1_clear` | 0.7 | — | no contact | correctly silent |
| `clean_b`, `clean_c`, `slip_a` | 0.4–0.9 | — | no contact | correctly silent |
| `clean_a` | 1.2 | 1.27s | no contact | **false positive** |

All three collisions found, on the joint that actually collided, at the right moment —
including the light `pair1` contact. `E3` detection no longer depends on a twin trajectory.

### Fix 1 — warm-up frames

Velocity, acceleration and lag features are zero-initialised, so for the first ~25 frames
the model predicts from history it does not have. Left in, `pair1_clear` — a run with **no
contact** — scored 2.2× at t=0.13s, and the same artefact outranked the genuine contacts in
`pair1` and `pair2`. A causal detector cannot score before its buffer fills; `WARMUP`
frames are now dropped at fit and eval alike.

### Fix 2 — the floor percentile is the operating point

At p95 every run reads as contact. At p99.9 `pair1` and `pair2` **still print CONTACT** —
but on `wrist_flex` at 9.19s and 9.33s, over a second from the real event. Coincidental
threshold crossings, which in a summary table would have looked like a clean 3/3.

Caught only by cross-checking peak time and joint against `causal_eval.py`'s matched-pair
output. That cross-check is now a documented required step, and `--floor-pct` exposes the
operating point rather than hiding it.

## 3. What this changes

- **Two High risks retired.** "Week 1 separation does not survive causal evaluation" —
  did not materialise. "No matched control exists at runtime" — addressed, with the
  caveats in §4 below.
- **D0's window is settled** at 5–10 frames, on measurement rather than taste, with a
  mechanism for the upper bound.

- **The long-window hypothesis moves to D0+**, where it belongs.
- **Corpus requirement added:** record grasp duration per episode.

## 4. What this does NOT establish

Read this before quoting any number above.

**The three positives are not three samples.** All three pairs replay the *same* recorded
episode — one trajectory, one joint, one corner of the workspace, three obstacle
placements. `pair3` is a 12 A hard stall visible in the raw trace without any model, so the
informative n is really 2.

**The threshold was chosen after seeing the answers.** The floor comes from held-out
free-space data, which is legitimate, but *which percentile* was picked by sweeping and
seeing which gave the best collision result. p99 is a hyperparameter fitted to seven runs.

**The false-alarm rate is unmeasured.** 1 false positive in 4 negatives — a 95% interval of
roughly 5–70%, which excludes nothing. And the negatives are the wrong kind: `clean_*` and
`slip_a` are teleoperated, `pair1_clear` is the only matched negative, and corpus negatives
will be *autonomous policy rollouts* — a third motion distribution the model has never seen.
For RQ4, false-alarm rate is the quantity that decides whether closed-loop recovery has
positive net gain.

**The margin is not large where it matters.** Weakest true positive 3.3×, strongest false
positive 1.2×. A factor of ~3, with seven runs behind it.

**Distribution shift is untested.** Trained on 113s of teleoperated free-space motion, one
payload, one session, one thermal state. Deployment is autonomous rollouts, varied
payloads, dozens of sessions, drifting servo temperature — and current draw is
temperature-dependent with no term in the model for it.

**The p99.9 near-miss is the strongest warning in the data**, because it actually happened
here rather than hypothetically: a verdict column can be right for the wrong reason.

This is a **signature test, not an accuracy estimate** — the same discipline
`WEEK1_REPORT.md` applies to the n=3 slip result. No AUPRC, false-alarm rate, or lead-time
distribution should be claimed from seven runs.

### What would earn confidence

1. **Hold out a whole trajectory** — fit free-space on trajectory A, test collisions on
   trajectory B. Single most informative next experiment; one session, no task objects.
2. **Matched pairs on different motions and different joints.** Everything so far is
   `shoulder_pan` on one sweep.
3. **Pre-register the floor percentile** before looking at any new collision data.
4. **Session-to-session refit test** — fit today, evaluate next week. Answers the
   temperature question for free.
5. **Negatives from autonomous rollouts**, once Arm 1 exists.

## 5. Artifacts

| file | role |
|---|---|
| `causal_eval.py` | trailing-window re-run of the Week 1 analysis; window sweep; centred-vs-trailing check |
| `freespace_model.py` | D0r — fits the free-space current model, scores residuals |

### Reproduce

```bash
conda activate lerobot

python research/telemetry/gate_analysis.py research/telemetry/runs \
    | tee research/telemetry/runs/offline_baseline.txt

python research/telemetry/causal_eval.py research/telemetry/runs \
    --sweep 3,5,10,15,30,50,150,500 \
    --report research/telemetry/runs/causal_sweep.csv --compare-offline

python research/telemetry/freespace_model.py fit \
    research/telemetry/runs/freespace_*.csv \
    --out research/telemetry/models/freespace.npz

python research/telemetry/freespace_model.py eval \
    research/telemetry/runs/pair1_clear.csv \
    research/telemetry/runs/pair*_obstacle.csv \
    research/telemetry/runs/clean_*.csv

# always sweep the operating point before believing a verdict
for p in 95 99 99.9; do
    python research/telemetry/freespace_model.py eval --floor-pct $p \
        research/telemetry/runs/pair*_obstacle.csv research/telemetry/runs/clean_*.csv
done
```
